Diffusion Models — From Noise to Samples

Make corruption easy to simulate, learn to undo small amounts of it, then turn a simple noise distribution into new data.

Reading path: this standalone derivation → diffusion language models → DiffusionGemma. Familiarity with Gaussians, expectations, and negative log-likelihood helps. Diffusion is a generative modeling method, not a particular neural-network layer.

1. Two processes, only one learned

Let \(x_0\) be a clean example and \(x_t\) its corrupted version at noise level \(t\). The forward process \(q\) deliberately destroys information. The reverse model \(p_\theta\) learns how plausible cleaner examples are distributed given a noisy one. At generation time, start from fresh noise—not a noisy copy of a training image—and repeatedly apply the learned reverse transitions.

StageAvailable informationOperation
Corrupt training dataClean \(x_0\), sampled noise, chosen \(t\)A fixed, known stochastic transformation.
Train the denoiserNoisy \(x_t\) and \(t\) as inputs; the clean example/noise as supervisionFit a neural network across noise levels.
GenerateFresh \(x_T\) and optional conditioning, but no clean targetIterate learned reverse updates to obtain \(x_0\).

Reversing corruption is not recovering a unique original. Many clean examples can explain the same noisy observation. A generative sampler must represent that uncertainty, rather than always returning a single conditional average.

2. Gaussian corruption and the shortcut to any timestep

In a variance-preserving DDPM, choose \(0<\beta_t<1\), let \(\alpha_t=1-\beta_t\), and define \(\bar\alpha_t=\prod_{s=1}^t\alpha_s\), with \(\bar\alpha_0=1\). A forward step scales the signal and adds independent Gaussian noise:

\[ q(x_t\mid x_{t-1})=\mathcal N(\sqrt{\alpha_t}\,x_{t-1},\,\beta_t I). \]

Substitution through the chain multiplies the signal coefficients. The accumulated noise variance is \(\sum_{s=1}^t\beta_s\prod_{j=s+1}^t\alpha_j=1-\bar\alpha_t\). Therefore the entire forward chain has a closed-form marginal:

\[ \boxed{x_t=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon, \qquad \epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I).} \]

This is why training does not need to execute all earlier corruption steps. If \(\bar\alpha_T\) is sufficiently small, the terminal distribution is approximately standard normal. The schedule controls the noise levels the network sees; the coefficient ratio \(\bar\alpha_t/(1-\bar\alpha_t)\) is the usual signal-to-noise ratio for unit-variance data.

3. Why predicting noise is a training objective

The generative model has joint density \(p_\theta(x_{0:T})=p(x_T)\prod_{t=1}^T p_\theta(x_{t-1}\mid x_t)\). Its marginal likelihood integrates out the noisy intermediate states. A variational bound turns that difficult likelihood into tractable terms:

\[ -\log p_\theta(x_0)\ \leq\ \mathbb E_q\!\left[ D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q(x_T\mid x_0)\Vert p(x_T)) +\sum_{t=2}^T D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q(x_{t-1}\mid x_t,x_0)\Vert p_\theta(x_{t-1}\mid x_t)) -\log p_\theta(x_0\mid x_1) \right]. \]

The crucial trick: the forward posterior \(q(x_{t-1}\mid x_t,x_0)\) is Gaussian and computable because training supplies \(x_0\). This does not give us the unconditional reverse transition for free at generation time.

For fixed reverse variance \(\sigma_t^2 I\), parameterize the reverse mean with a noise predictor \(\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)\):

\[ \mu_\theta(x_t,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}} \left(x_t-\frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}\,\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)\right). \] \[ L_{t-1}= \mathbb E\!\left[ \frac{\beta_t^2}{2\sigma_t^2\alpha_t(1-\bar\alpha_t)} \|\epsilon-\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)\|^2 \right]+\text{constant},\qquad t>1. \]

The DDPM paper's widely used simplified objective drops this timestep-dependent weight and trains with uniform timestep sampling:

\[ \mathcal L_{\mathrm{simple}} =\mathbb E_{x_0,\,t\sim U\{1,\ldots,T\},\,\epsilon} \|\epsilon-\epsilon_\theta(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0+ \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\epsilon,t)\|^2. \]
MSE is not the exact negative log-likelihood. It comes from a particular parameterization and reweighting of a variational objective. Noise prediction, clean-data prediction, and velocity prediction are different parameterizations; their loss weights and numerical conditioning matter.

One training update, in pseudocode:

x0 = sample_training_batch()
t = sample_uniform_timesteps(1, T, per_example=True)
epsilon = sample_standard_normal_like(x0)
xt = sqrt(alpha_bar[t]) * x0 + sqrt(1 - alpha_bar[t]) * epsilon
prediction = denoiser(xt, t)
loss = mean_squared_error(prediction, epsilon)
update_parameters(loss)

The timestep coefficients must broadcast over the example's non-batch dimensions. This update uses one denoiser evaluation per example's sampled noise level, not backpropagation through an entire sampling chain. A U-Net or transformer can implement the denoiser; diffusion does not require either one.

4. Sampling: fresh noise, then many learned updates

Initialize \(x_T\sim\mathcal N(0,I)\). For \(t=T,\ldots,1\), evaluate the denoiser and update:

\[ x_{t-1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}} \left(x_t-\frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)\right) +\sigma_t z. \]

Here \(z\sim\mathcal N(0,I)\) for \(t>1\), and the usual sampling algorithm sets \(z=0\) on the final step. One fixed variance choice is the forward posterior variance \(\sigma_t^2=\tilde\beta_t=\beta_t(1-\bar\alpha_{t-1})/(1-\bar\alpha_t)\). The random term preserves uncertainty along the chain; subtracting a predicted noise vector once is not the DDPM sampler.

The noise-prediction optimum is a conditional expectation, not an oracle that can identify the exact noise used for every training example. The many-step stochastic generative process is what turns those local predictions into a distribution of outputs.

Training steps and sampling steps are different budgets. A gradient update learns from a sampled noise level. Producing one new example requires repeated network calls. A faster sampler can change that inference budget without proportionally changing the training cost.

5. Score matching, faster samplers, and conditioning

The score interpretation

The score is the gradient of the log density with respect to the noisy data, \(s_t(x)=\nabla_x\log q_t(x)\), not a gradient with respect to network parameters. For Gaussian corruption and \(t>0\), the optimal noise predictor satisfies:

\[ s_t(x_t)=-\frac{\mathbb E[\epsilon\mid x_t]}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}} \ \approx\ -\frac{\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}. \]

This connects denoising to following density information at many noise levels. It does not mean that a deterministic gradient ascent to one density mode is a correct generative sampler.

DDIM changes the sampling path

DDIM constructs a different reverse process compatible with the same denoising training objective. It can use a subset of noise levels and, with its stochasticity parameter set to zero, a deterministic trajectory conditional on the initial random noise. Fewer evaluations trade accuracy against speed; simply skipping DDPM loop iterations without changing the update is not DDIM.

Classifier-free guidance changes the conditional distribution

Train with conditioning \(c\) sometimes dropped, then combine conditional and unconditional noise predictions at sampling time:

\[ \hat\epsilon=\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t,\varnothing) +w\,[\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t,c)-\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t,\varnothing)]. \]

In this convention \(w=1\) gives the conditional prediction; \(w>1\) strengthens guidance. Stronger guidance can improve condition adherence while sacrificing diversity or creating artifacts. It is neither free computation nor an exact sample from the original unguided conditional model.

6. The same idea, different state spaces

Image diffusion may operate on pixels or compressed continuous latents. A diffusion policy denoises continuous action sequences conditioned on observations; its execution horizon and closed-loop behavior are separate concerns.

Text token IDs are categorical, not coordinates where adding Gaussian noise has semantic meaning. The next page replaces the corruption law with token masking or random-token replacement, and replaces noise regression with categorical denoising. Continuous-embedding text diffusion is another possible route, not the discrete-token method derived there.
