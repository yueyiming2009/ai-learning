PPO & GRPO — Step-by-Step Backward Pass

Start at the scalar loss. Differentiate the clip, the ratio, and the KL term. Carry the result through log-softmax to every vocabulary logit, then into the model weights.

Companion to PPO and GRPO. The policy gradient theorem explains why a rollout can supply a learning signal. Here we answer a different question: given a sampled batch and its advantages, what exactly does loss.backward() calculate?

1. Fix the batch before differentiating

First consider one sampled token \(a\) in its fixed prompt/prefix \(s\). Assume a categorical policy with finite logits, unit temperature, and the same action support for the learner and the recorded old policy. All probabilities below refer to that same \(a,s\).

QuantityMeaningReceives gradients in this actor update?
\(\mathbf z_\theta\in\mathbb R^V,\;\mathbf p=\operatorname{softmax}(\mathbf z)\)Current logits and probabilitiesYes
\(\ell=\log p_a\)Current sampled-token log-probabilityYes
\(\ell_{\rm old}=\log\pi_{\rm old}(a\mid s)\)Stored rollout-policy log-probabilityNo
\(\ell_{\rm ref}=\log\pi_{\rm ref}(a\mid s)\)Frozen reference log-probabilityNo
\(A,\;a,\;s\)Advantage, sampled token ID, prefix token IDsNo
\(\epsilon,\;\beta,\;w\)Clip width, KL coefficient, reduction weightNo; \(0<\epsilon<1,\;\beta,w\geq0\)

PPO typically obtains \(A\) from GAE; GRPO obtains it from a group's rewards, for example \(A_i=(R_i-\operatorname{mean}_j R_j)/(\operatorname{std}_j R_j+\delta)\). Both are frozen numbers during the actor backward pass. GRPO usually broadcasts \(A_i\) to all response tokens of answer \(i\).

Frozen token IDs do not mean frozen model activations. The learner recomputes a differentiable forward pass on the recorded sequence. Gradients flow through its embeddings, attention, and MLPs, but not through the discrete sampling operation or the reward computation. Never recompute \(\ell_{\rm old}\) from the live actor inside this graph: differentiating \(\exp(\ell-\ell)\) would give zero.

2. Forward pass — write the loss we will minimize

For one token, compute:

\[ \ell = z_a-\log\sum_{j=1}^{V}e^{z_j},\qquad \rho=e^{\ell-\ell_{\rm old}},\qquad u=e^{\ell_{\rm ref}-\ell}. \]

\(\rho=\pi_\theta/\pi_{\rm old}\) controls the policy surrogate; \(u=\pi_{\rm ref}/\pi_\theta\) belongs to the reference-KL estimator. They are different ratios, in opposite directions.

Per-token loss — gradient descent, not objective ascent
\[ L_{\rm token} = \underbrace{-\min\!\left(A\rho,\; A\,\operatorname{clip}(\rho,1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)\right)}_{L_{\rm PG}} + \underbrace{\beta\,(u-\log u-1)}_{L_{\rm KL}}. \]

Set \(\beta=0\) for the clipped actor loss alone. The explicit \(k_3\) KL term is the GRPO-style choice considered here; not every PPO/GRPO implementation enables it. A PPO setup that puts KL into rewards instead has already folded that information into the detached advantages. Do not add a second KL loss unless that is intentional.

For a batch, \(L_{\rm actor}=\sum_{i,t}w_{i,t}L_{{\rm token},i,t}\). We derive one token with \(w=1\) first, then restore masks and normalization in §9. The backward graph is:

L_token
  ├─ L_PG → min / clip → rho = exp(ell - old_ell) ─┐
  └─ L_KL → u - log(u) - 1 → u = exp(ref_ell - ell) ┤
                                                   ↓
                            ell = log_softmax(z)[a] → z → model weights

3. Backward step 1 — differentiate the min and clip

Let \(l=1-\epsilon\) and \(h=1+\epsilon\). Start by resolving the minimum before taking derivatives. The sign of \(A\) determines which side flattens.

Positive advantage: upper plateau

If \(A>0\), then for \(\rho<l\), \(A\rho<Al\), so the raw branch wins. Inside the band both branches equal \(A\rho\). For \(\rho>h\), \(Ah<A\rho\), so the clipped branch wins. Therefore:

\[ L_{\rm PG}= \begin{cases}-A\rho & \rho<h\\-Ah & \rho>h\end{cases}, \qquad \frac{\partial L_{\rm PG}}{\partial\rho}= \begin{cases}-A & \rho<h\\0 & \rho>h.\end{cases} \]
Negative advantage: lower plateau

If \(A<0\), multiplication reverses the comparisons. Below \(l\), \(Al<A\rho\), so the clipped branch wins. Inside the band the branches coincide. Above \(h\), \(A\rho<Ah\), so the raw branch wins:

\[ L_{\rm PG}= \begin{cases}-Al & \rho<l\\-A\rho & \rho>l\end{cases}, \qquad \frac{\partial L_{\rm PG}}{\partial\rho}= \begin{cases}0 & \rho<l\\-A & \rho>l.\end{cases} \]

Define an active-branch indicator \(C\), away from the kink points:

\[ C(A,\rho)= \begin{cases} 1 & A>0\ \text{and}\ \rho<1+\epsilon,\\ 1 & A<0\ \text{and}\ \rho>1-\epsilon,\\ 0 & \text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \qquad \frac{\partial L_{\rm PG}}{\partial\rho}=-A\,C. \]
Outside the clip interval does not always mean zero gradient. A good action whose probability fell too far, or a bad action whose probability rose too far, still uses the raw branch. Only the advantage-improving side plateaus. At \(A=0\) the policy-loss gradient is zero everywhere. At the active plateau boundary the loss is not differentiable; autograd picks an operation-specific subgradient. The formulas, plot, and numerical checks here use the open regions, not a claimed unique derivative at a kink.

4. Backward step 2 — ratio to log-probability

The exponential has a particularly simple derivative. Since \(\ell_{\rm old}\) is constant:

\[ \frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\ell} = e^{\ell-\ell_{\rm old}}=\rho. \]

Multiply the upstream derivative from §3 by this local derivative:

Policy-loss gradient arriving at the sampled log-probability
\[ g_{\rm PG}\equiv\frac{\partial L_{\rm PG}}{\partial\ell} =\frac{\partial L_{\rm PG}}{\partial\rho} \frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\ell} =-A\rho C. \]

This is not \(-A\,\operatorname{clip}(\rho)\). In a plateau it is zero; in an active region it uses the unclipped ratio. At \(\rho=1\), \(C=1\) for nonzero \(A\), giving \(-A\). Although the ratio's value is 1 when the policies match, its derivative is not zero: the numerator is live, the denominator is frozen.

5. Backward step 3 — the reference-KL branch

Differentiate the sampled \(k_3\) term on the same frozen token:

\[ \frac{\partial u}{\partial\ell}=-u,\qquad \frac{\partial (u-\log u-1)}{\partial u}=1-\frac1u. \]
Chain rule, then sum the two branches
\[ g_{\rm KL} =\beta\left(1-\frac1u\right)(-u) =\beta(1-u), \qquad g\equiv\frac{\partial L_{\rm token}}{\partial\ell} =-A\rho C+\beta(1-u). \]

If \(p_a>p_{{\rm ref},a}\), then \(u<1\) and the KL contribution is positive: gradient descent pulls the sampled log-probability down. If \(p_a<p_{{\rm ref},a}\), it is negative and pulls up. Even when \(C=0\), the KL gradient can remain nonzero.

Which KL gradient did we just compute? This is the exact derivative of the finite-batch, sampled \(k_3\) loss with the sampling distribution held fixed. The identity \(\mathbb E_{a\sim\pi_\theta}[k_3]=\mathrm{KL}(\pi_\theta\|\pi_{\rm ref})\) does not make its detached-sample backward an unbiased gradient of that moving expectation: differentiating the expectation also produces \(\mathbb E_{\pi_\theta}[k_3\,\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta]\). Reused old-policy samples introduce a further distribution mismatch. This page derives the implemented surrogate, not an exact gradient of expected task reward or of the full current-policy KL.
The policy contribution is -A times the ratio in the active region and zero in the plateau. The total adds beta times (1 - 1/ratio) when reference and old policies agree.

Clipping switches off one branch, not the whole backward pass. Horizontal: \(\rho\). Vertical: \(\partial L/\partial\ell\), not \(\partial L/\partial\rho\). 6. Backward step 4 — log-probability to every vocabulary logit

Now forget how \(g\) was produced. It is the upstream scalar arriving at \(\ell=z_a-\log\sum_k e^{z_k}\). Differentiate with respect to any logit \(z_j\):

\[ \frac{\partial\ell}{\partial z_j} =\underbrace{\frac{\partial z_a}{\partial z_j}}_{\mathbf1[j=a]} -\underbrace{\frac{e^{z_j}}{\sum_k e^{z_k}}}_{p_j} =\mathbf1[j=a]-p_j. \]
Full per-token logit gradient
\[ \boxed{\frac{\partial L_{\rm token}}{\partial z_j} =\left[-A\rho C+\beta(1-u)\right] \left(\mathbf1[j=a]-p_j\right)} \] \[ \nabla_{\mathbf z}L_{\rm token}=g\,(\mathbf e_a-\mathbf p). \]

The gather selects only \(a\), but the log-softmax denominator depends on every logit. Unselected vocabulary entries therefore receive gradients too. Their sum is zero: \(g(1-\sum_jp_j)=0\), consistent with softmax being unchanged by adding the same constant to every logit.

Compare cross entropy, \(L_{\rm CE}=-\ell\), whose upstream scalar is \(-1\) and whose gradient is \(\mathbf p-\mathbf e_a\). At \(\rho=1,\beta=0\), PPO/GRPO gives \(A(\mathbf p-\mathbf e_a)\): signed, advantage-weighted cross entropy at the sampled token. This is equality of the gradients at that point, not equality of the scalar PPO and cross-entropy losses.

7. Backward step 5 — logits to parameters

Write the final projection with row-vector activations: \(\mathbf z=\mathbf hW+\mathbf b\), where \(\mathbf h\in\mathbb R^{1\times d}\), \(W\in\mathbb R^{d\times V}\). Given \(d\mathbf z=g(\mathbf e_a-\mathbf p)\), the linear backward is:

\[ dW=\mathbf h^\top d\mathbf z,\qquad d\mathbf b=d\mathbf z,\qquad d\mathbf h=d\mathbf z\,W^\top. \]

For \(N\) flattened batch/token positions: \(dW=H^\top dZ\), \(d\mathbf b=\sum_{n=1}^{N}dZ_n\), \(dH=dZ\,W^\top\). The loss reduction weights are already included in \(dZ\). Omit the bias term if the language-model head has no bias.

The transformer then receives \(dH\) and applies the same local backward rules as in SFT: RMSNorm, SwiGLU, attention, and residual paths. Shared parameters accumulate contributions from every token and branch; tied embeddings additionally receive their input-embedding contribution. Backpropagation / VJPs derives this composition.

Parameter-space form of the same backward pass
\[ \nabla_\theta L_{\rm actor} =\sum_{i,t}w_{i,t} \left[-A_{i,t}\rho_{i,t}C_{i,t} +\beta(1-u_{i,t})\right] \nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_{i,t}\mid s_{i,t}). \]

Negate this for objective ascent. In the unclipped \(\rho=1\) case it becomes the \([A+\beta(u-1)]\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta\) expression on GRPO §6. Gradient clipping by norm and the optimizer update happen after this derivative; PPO ratio clipping is a different operation.

8. Worked backward — three vocabulary entries

Use \(\mathbf z=(\log0.5,\log0.3,\log0.2)\), so \(\mathbf p=(0.5,0.3,0.2)\). The sampled token is the first entry, \(\mathbf e_a-\mathbf p=(0.5,-0.3,-0.2)\). Set \(A=2,\epsilon=0.2,\beta=0.1,p_{{\rm ref},a}=0.4,w=1\).

Case A — no clipping: old probability = 0.5
\[ \rho=0.5/0.5=1,\quad C=1,\quad u=0.4/0.5=0.8. \] \[ L_{\rm PG}=-2,\quad L_{\rm KL}=0.1(0.8-\log0.8-1)\approx0.002314. \] \[ \frac{\partial L_{\rm PG}}{\partial\rho}=-2,\quad \frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\ell}=1,\quad g_{\rm PG}=-2,\quad g_{\rm KL}=0.02,\quad g=-1.98. \] \[ d\mathbf z=-1.98(0.5,-0.3,-0.2)=(-0.99,\;0.594,\;0.396). \]

A direct SGD step on these logits with \(\eta=0.01\) adds \((0.0099,-0.00594,-0.00396)\), increasing the sampled token's probability.

Case B — good action past its plateau: old probability = 0.4
\[ \rho=1.25>1.2,\quad C=0,\quad L_{\rm PG}=-\min(2.5,2.4)=-2.4. \] \[ g_{\rm PG}=0,\quad g_{\rm KL}=0.02,\quad d\mathbf z=0.02(0.5,-0.3,-0.2)=(0.01,-0.006,-0.004). \]

The policy branch stops rewarding further increase; the KL branch still pulls toward the reference. If \(\beta=0\), this token contributes exactly zero actor gradient.

9. Masks and averaging — where PPO and GRPO differ

The local derivative is the same for both algorithms once \(A\) is fixed. Advantage construction and the loss's reduction weights determine which contributions get emphasized. Let \(m_{i,t}\) be 1 for an included response token and 0 otherwise, \(T_i=\sum_t m_{i,t}\), and assume every included answer has \(T_i>0\).

ReductionWeight \(w_{i,t}\)Consequence
Token mean over the batch\(m_{i,t}/\sum_{j,s}m_{j,s}\)Equal weight per included token
Original GRPO: answer mean, then prompt/group mean\(m_{i,t}/(BGT_i)\), for \(B\) prompts, \(G\) answers eachEqual total weight per answer, hence larger per-token weights for short answers
Dr.GRPO-style constant divisor\(m_{i,t}/(BG T_{\max})\), fixed \(T_{\max}\)No inverse-realized-length weighting; total scale depends on token count
\[ \frac{\partial L_{\rm actor}}{\partial z_{i,t,j}} =w_{i,t}\,g_{i,t}\left(\mathbf1[j=a_{i,t}]-p_{i,t,j}\right). \]

For finite per-token computations, \(m=0\) removes that token's direct loss contribution, not all gradient paths through its representation: prompt tokens can still influence later response predictions through attention. If all rewards in a GRPO group are identical, its normalized advantages are zero, so its policy-loss contribution vanishes; an explicit KL or entropy term need not vanish.

Handle empty-response batches explicitly rather than divide by zero. Sanitize or exclude invalid padded computations before exponentiation: multiplying an infinite loss by a zero mask does not make it finite. Gradient accumulation and distributed reductions must preserve the intended denominator; averaging unequal-size microbatch means is not a global token mean. See GRPO loss aggregation in practice.

10. What about PPO's critic and entropy bonus?

The derivation above is the actor's clipped surrogate plus optional sampled reference-KL. For the illustrative full loss \(L_{\rm total}=L_{\rm actor}+c_V L_V-c_H H\), additional branches contribute:

TermLocal backwardBoundary
Unclipped value regression \(L_V=\frac12(V_\psi-R_{\rm target})^2\)\(\partial(c_VL_V)/\partial V_\psi=c_V(V_\psi-R_{\rm target})\)Target is detached; clipped value-loss variants have their own branches
Exact categorical entropy \(H=-\sum_jp_j\log p_j\)\(\partial(-c_HH)/\partial z_j=c_Hp_j(\log p_j+H)\)A full-vocabulary entropy, not a detached sampled surprisal

For entropy, apply the softmax Jacobian to \(\partial H/\partial p_k=-(\log p_k+1)\): \(\partial H/\partial z_j=-p_j(\log p_j+H)\); the loss has another minus sign. Apply each term's configured reduction separately. A separate PPO critic updates its own parameters; a shared actor/critic trunk sums their gradients. GRPO removes the critic, not the actor's ordinary transformer backward. See the full PPO objective and policy entropy.

11. Reference code — autograd versus the derived backward

This small PyTorch example implements exactly the loss above, not a full trainer. Logits have shape [B, T, V]; the other tensors have shape [B, T]. Old/reference log-probs, advantages, and reduction weights are detached. The manual derivative intentionally excludes exact clip-boundary inputs.

import torch

def actor_loss_and_manual_grad(z, actions, old_logp, ref_logp, advantages,
                               weights, eps=0.2, beta=0.1):
    old_logp, ref_logp = old_logp.detach(), ref_logp.detach()
    advantages, weights = advantages.detach(), weights.detach()
    logp_all = z.log_softmax(dim=-1)
    logp = logp_all.gather(-1, actions.unsqueeze(-1)).squeeze(-1)
    ratio = (logp - old_logp).exp()
    delta = ref_logp - logp
    k3 = delta.expm1() - delta
    pg = -torch.minimum(ratio * advantages,
                        ratio.clamp(1 - eps, 1 + eps) * advantages)
    loss = (weights * (pg + beta * k3)).sum()

    with torch.no_grad():
        active = ((advantages > 0) & (ratio < 1 + eps)) | (
                  (advantages < 0) & (ratio > 1 - eps))
        g = weights * (-advantages * ratio * active - beta * delta.expm1())
        dz = -g.unsqueeze(-1) * logp_all.exp()
        dz.scatter_add_(-1, actions.unsqueeze(-1), g.unsqueeze(-1))
    return loss, dz

dtype = torch.float64
z = torch.tensor([0.5, 0.3, 0.2], dtype=dtype).log()
z = z.repeat(1, 7, 1).requires_grad_()
actions = torch.zeros((1, 7), dtype=torch.long)
ratios = torch.tensor([[0.7, 1.0, 1.3, 0.7, 1.0, 1.3, 1.0]], dtype=dtype)
old_logp = (0.5 / ratios).log()
ref_logp = torch.full((1, 7), 0.4, dtype=dtype).log()
advantages = torch.tensor([[2, 2, 2, -2, -2, -2, 9]], dtype=dtype)
weights = torch.tensor([[1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 0]], dtype=dtype) / 6

loss, manual_dz = actor_loss_and_manual_grad(
    z, actions, old_logp, ref_logp, advantages, weights)
loss.backward()
torch.testing.assert_close(z.grad, manual_dz, rtol=1e-10, atol=1e-12)
torch.testing.assert_close(z.grad.sum(-1), torch.zeros_like(weights),
                           rtol=0, atol=1e-12)
assert torch.count_nonzero(z.grad[0, -1]) == 0
print("Manual backward matches autograd.")

expm1(delta) - delta evaluates \(e^\delta-1-\delta\) with better near-zero numerical behavior than subtracting 1 from exp(delta). Its derivative is still \(1-u=-\operatorname{expm1}(\delta)\) with respect to \(\ell\). Production code also needs stable precision, finite-value handling, exact rollout/learner probability contracts, and its configured loss variant. Clamping log-ratios or detaching additional factors changes the derivative; do not silently substitute those operations.

12. The entire backward pass in five lines

Backward edgeResult for one token
Loss → ratio\(\partial L_{\rm PG}/\partial\rho=-AC\)
Ratio + KL → log-probability\(g=-A\rho C+\beta(1-u)\)
Log-probability → logits\(d\mathbf z=wg(\mathbf e_a-\mathbf p)\)
Logits → head weights\(dW=\mathbf h^\top d\mathbf z\)
Logits → transformer\(d\mathbf h=d\mathbf zW^\top\), then ordinary backprop

The RL-specific part is the scalar \(g\) and its reduction weight. Everything below log-softmax reuses the same model derivatives as supervised learning. For sequence-level ratios, dual clipping, or detached importance weights, rederive \(g\) from that actual forward loss rather than reusing the vanilla PPO gate.
