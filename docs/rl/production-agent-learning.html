Agentic RL · Production

Learning from Real Users

How to connect a deployed agent to training without turning customers into an uncontrolled exploration environment: the maturity ladder, production data flywheel, reward boundary, and research frontier.

A deployed agent generates the richest possible training data: real tasks, real tool failures, real preferences, and long-horizon outcomes. Connecting that experience to training is not a crazy idea—it is the natural destination of agent learning. The dangerous version is narrower: letting an updating policy experiment directly on users and publish its own weight changes.

Learn from real users; do not let an unconstrained learner experiment on them.
Production experience should enter a gated learning loop, not a direct gradient-to-deployment pipe.

1. "Connected to Training" Means Five Different Things

Discussions often collapse observability, personalization, batch training, online experimentation, and continuous weight updates into one phrase. They have very different risk profiles.

1

Observe

Versioned production traces capture prompts, tool calls, outcomes, latency, errors, and consent state. Nothing learns yet.

2

Adapt without weights

Memory, retrieval, prompt selection, routing, and workflow configuration adapt immediately while the model remains frozen.

3

Train asynchronously

Curated production trajectories become an offline SFT, preference-learning, or RL dataset. A candidate policy is produced later.

4

Experiment behind gates

Candidates run in replay, shadow, canary, or randomized trials with guardrail metrics and rollback.

5

Update weights online

The serving policy learns continuously from fresh interactions. This is still a frontier architecture for general-purpose LLM agents.

Immediate personalization is common, but it usually changes context, not weights. A memory entry can be deleted and audited; a learned behavioral tendency is distributed across parameters and much harder to reverse.

2. Where Research and Industry Are

PatternMaturityTypical useMain boundary
Logged feedback → periodic retraining Established Conversations, thumbs, edits, accepted outputs, support outcomes Consent, privacy filtering, curation, and delayed deployment
Contextual bandits Mature in bounded domains Ranking, recommendation, ads, notification and policy selection Known action set, propensity logging, constrained exploration
Production-trace agent optimization Emerging Prompt/workflow tuning, model routing, SFT and batch RL Trace schema, reward attribution, replay and evaluation
Agent RL in realistic environments Active research Coding, search, SQL, retrieval, browser and tool-use tasks Usually sandboxed tasks or controlled datasets—not live customers
Continuous live weight learning Frontier Long-lived adaptive agents Safe exploration, reward integrity, regression control, rollback

Silver and Sutton's Era of Experience makes the research case: persistent agents should learn from streams of grounded experience, actions, observations, and consequences rather than only static human-generated data. Agent Lightning supplies part of the systems bridge by turning executions from existing agent frameworks into transitions for a separate RL trainer. Its published examples remain controlled tasks; richer user feedback and more advanced online methods are presented as future work.

Public product policies show the prevailing deployment pattern. For example, OpenAI's data controls describe opted-in conversations and submitted feedback as material that may improve later models. That is a data flywheel, not evidence that an individual interaction immediately updates the model serving the next user.

3. The Production-Safe Learning Loop

Serve

Frozen, versioned policy handles real tasks.

Curate

Consent, PII filtering, rewards, and trace quality.

Learn

Offline replay, SFT, preference learning, or batch RL.

Gate

Replay → shadow → canary → promotion or rollback.

Trace contract

Every production trajectory needs enough provenance to be replayable and interpretable:

  • policy, prompt, harness, tool, and environment versions;
  • observations, model actions, tool results, timing, retries, and terminal state;
  • sampling metadata when policy-gradient training is intended;
  • consent and retention status plus PII/security classifications;
  • immediate feedback and delayed business or task outcomes;
  • exposure propensity when the system chose among alternatives.

The harnessed agentic RL boundary is useful here: the agent runtime owns real execution, while an event/trajectory layer gives the trainer a stable contract. Production serving and training can share the trace format without sharing a failure domain.

Promotion gates

Replay

Run the candidate against immutable historical tasks. Detect regressions and compare counterfactual predictions where possible.

Offline eval

Require capability, safety, privacy, and adversarial suites—not only the optimized reward.

Shadow

Observe what the candidate would do without exposing its actions to users or tools.

Canary

Expose a small, controlled population with bounded actions, stop conditions, and automatic rollback.

Promote

A separate controller—not the learner—changes the production policy after explicit acceptance criteria pass.

4. Real Users Produce Signals, Not a Ready-Made Reward

Strong signals

Verified task completion, accepted code, tests passing, resolved ticket, successful transaction, durable correction.

Useful but incomplete

User edits, retries, escalation, abandonment, explicit preference, time-to-completion, downstream reuse.

Easy to misread

Thumbs, clicks, dwell time, conversation length, immediate satisfaction, agent self-reports.

Unsafe alone

Revenue, engagement, or retention optimized without truthfulness, fairness, safety, and user-welfare constraints.

User feedback is affected by who chooses to respond, what action they were shown, whether the task was inherently difficult, and whether the consequence appears hours or weeks later. A production reward therefore needs multiple channels and explicit constraints:

ChannelWhat it capturesFailure mode
Outcome verifierDid the task actually succeed?Proxy can miss quality, side effects, or hidden requirements.
User feedbackWas the result useful or preferred?Selection bias, politeness, manipulation, short-term preference.
Process constraintsDid the agent obey policy and tool boundaries?Checklist compliance can become the objective instead of the task.
Delayed outcomeDid the work remain correct and valuable?Sparse signal and difficult credit assignment.
Guardrail metricsSafety, privacy, fairness, cost, latencyAverages can hide subgroup or tail regressions.

5. Why a Direct Live Loop Fails

Reward hacking

The agent learns to satisfy the measurement rather than the user's underlying goal.

User manipulation

If satisfaction or engagement is reward, persuasion and sycophancy can become instrumental strategies.

Biased feedback

Only selected actions receive outcomes; vocal users and common tasks dominate the gradient.

Policy lag

Logged behavior comes from older policies, tools, and prompts, making naive updates off-policy.

Catastrophic regression

A local gain can erase rare capabilities or safety behavior across the entire user population.

Privacy leakage

Training can memorize sensitive user or enterprise data unless collection and retention are designed for learning.

An agent may be able to affect the same system that produces its reward. Anthropic's reward-tampering experiments show why the reward channel, evaluator, policy deployment, and training controller should be separate trust domains.

6. What Remains Research

  • Long-horizon credit assignment: which model calls among dozens caused the final outcome?
  • Off-policy correction: how should learning account for trajectories generated by older or different production policies?
  • Safe exploration: how can an agent discover better behavior without imposing unacceptable actions on users?
  • Counterfactual evaluation: would another action have produced a better result when only one was actually served?
  • Continual learning without forgetting: can adaptation preserve rare skills, safety behavior, and calibrated uncertainty?
  • Reward integrity: can the agent influence users, evaluators, logs, tools, or the metric itself?
  • Consent and deletion: how do data removal and purpose limitations propagate through datasets, checkpoints, and derived models?

A practical sequencing rule

Start hereAdvance whenDo not advance if
Production traces → offline training Trace quality, consent, and outcome labeling are reliable You cannot replay or attribute the data to a policy version
Offline candidate → canary Independent evals and guardrails pass with rollback ready The optimized metric is the only acceptance test
Bandit optimization Actions are bounded, reversible, and propensity-logged Exploration can create material user or business harm
Online weight updates All prior controls work and update-level rollback is proven The learner can publish itself or alter its reward channel
The near-term production pattern is a real-experience data flywheel with batch learning and gated deployment. Fully online agent-weight learning is the frontier, not the default.
