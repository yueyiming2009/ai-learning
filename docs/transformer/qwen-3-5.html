Model Architectures

Qwen3.5

From the hybrid architecture to the training cluster: count every decoder layer, separate routed compute from resident weights, and choose an SFT sharding strategy for the 27B, 35B-A3B, or 397B-A17B model.

Qwen3.5 (February 2026, Apache 2.0) is the generation where Alibaba's hybrid-attention experiment became the flagship. Its architecture was not invented at 397B — it was validated at 80B first (Qwen3-Next, September 2025), then scaled 5× with almost no structural change. That makes Qwen3.5 the cleanest public example of how frontier labs now de-risk architecture: prove the recipe on a testbed model, then spend the real compute on a known-good shape.

The full release spans 0.8B → 397B (dense up to 27B, MoE above) on one architecture, plus the hosted Qwen3.5-Plus. No standalone tech report had appeared as of this writing — the spec below follows the model card, the released config.json, and the official blog.

Reading this as a training guide

1. The Testbed Pattern — Qwen3-Next Was the Experiment

Qwen3-Next (80B-A3B) introduced every load-bearing idea: the 3:1 hybrid attention layout, output-gated attention, the 512-expert high-sparsity MoE, MTP, partial RoPE, and a package of numerics-stability fixes (zero-centered RMSNorm, weight decay on norm gains). Its headline is the same pattern at a smaller scale: the 125B-A6B testbed matches the 397B-A17B flagship on 8 of 14 pre-training benchmarks at roughly a ninth of the training FLOPs.

The one genuinely new capability is multimodality (§7). Everything else is the testbed recipe with more of everything — which is itself the result: the hybrid architecture survived a 5× scale-up unmodified, the strongest evidence yet that linear-attention hybrids are not a small-model curiosity.

2. The Hybrid Stack — Why 3:1

Of Qwen3.5's 60 layers, 45 mix tokens with Gated DeltaNet — a fixed-size recurrent memory, \(O(1)\) per token — and 15 with softmax attention (full_attention_interval: 4: every fourth layer). The split assigns each mechanism what it is best at:

  • DeltaNet layers carry the bulk of sequence modeling. Most token mixing — local syntax, topic tracking, incremental state — does not need exact retrieval over the entire history. A linear-attention state handles it at constant per-token cost and constant memory.
  • Full-attention layers restore exact recall. A fixed-size state is lossy by information theory — verbatim recall over long spans (a needle in 200K tokens of haystack) can't live there. Fifteen softmax layers, each seeing the entire uncompressed history, provide the exact random-access path. They are the expensive 25% that make the cheap 75% safe.
full attention in all 60 layers Qwen3.5 hybrid (15 KV layers + state) DeltaNet state floor (constant)

The hybrid pays a fixed state cost to cut the per-token cost 4×. Total sequence memory across the stack (BF16), log–log. The 45 DeltaNet layers hold a constant ≈94 MB of recurrent state (64 heads × 128×128 per layer) regardless of context — which is why the hybrid line starts above the full-attention line and stays there until ≈1K tokens. Past the crossover, only 15 of 60 layers accumulate KV cache, so the hybrid grows at ¼ the slope: at the native 262K context it holds ≈8 GB where full attention would hold ≈32 GB, and the gap is one contributor to the reported decode-throughput advantage at 256K, not a prediction of SFT speed.

This is an inference-cache illustration, not a training-memory estimate. It assumes BF16 recurrent state and omits the small convolution cache; the pinned configs now specify mamba_ssm_dtype: float32, which doubles the recurrent-state term to ≈189 MB in backends honoring that setting. Training also retains activations or recomputes them for backward. A fixed-size decode state does not imply constant-memory training.

The ratio is a capacity bet, not a law. Qwen3-Next and Qwen3.5 use 3:1; Kimi K3 independently landed on 3:1 (KDA : gated MLA); MiniMax's earlier hybrid used 7:1. Published ablations from the Kimi Linear line found 3:1 the quality-neutral sweet spot — more full layers waste compute, fewer starts to cost recall. That two labs converged independently is the field's current best empirical answer. The full design pattern — ratio economics, placement rules, serving consequences — is on the hybrid attention page.

3. Inside the DeltaNet Layers

The mechanism — delta-rule overwrite plus learned forgetting, \(S_t = \alpha_t(I - \beta_t k_t k_t^\top)S_{t-1} + \beta_t k_t v_t^\top\) — is derived step by step on the Gated DeltaNet page, including the exact decode loop and the chunked kernel that trains it. What the 397B config adds:

  • 64 value heads × 16 QK heads, head dim 128 — each \((q, k)\) pair serves four value heads, and each value head owns a 128 × 128 state. Doubled from the testbed's 32 value heads: at fixed head dim, state capacity scales with head count, and a 5× bigger model needs a bigger memory.
  • Depthwise causal conv1d (kernel 4) after the input \(q,k,v\) projections and before QK normalization / the delta rule — the cheap local-mixing preprocessor.
  • A per-head gated RMSNorm on the way out — the output gate that lets a head silence its own readout.

4. The Gated Attention Layers

The 15 softmax layers are not vanilla either. Config: GQA with 32 query / 2 KV heads at head dim 256 (a large head dim and an aggressively small KV group count — 1024 KV elements per token per layer), with two modifications:

  • A sigmoid output gate on attention. Each layer computes \(o_t = W_O\bigl[\sigma(W_g x_t) \odot \mathrm{Attn}(x_t)\bigr]\) — an input-dependent, per-channel gate between the attention read and the output projection. The gated-attention line of work (Qwen's own "Gated Attention" paper) found this kills two chronic pathologies at once: attention sinks (heads dumping probability on the first token because softmax must sum to 1 — the gate gives them a way to output nothing instead) and massive activations (outlier channels that wreck low-precision inference). DeepSeek-V4 reaches the same destination with an explicit sink logit in the softmax; Kimi K3 adopts the same sigmoid gate on top of MLA.
  • Partial RoPE: only ¼ of each head's dims rotate (64 of 256), at \(\theta = 10^7\). The unrotated dims are position-free content channels; the long wavelength keeps the rotated dims slow enough to extrapolate. Same compromise as DeepSeek-V4 (64 of 512), one step short of Kimi K3's full NoPE on global layers — across all three frontier models, positional signal is in retreat.

Stability details inherited from Qwen3-Next — zero-centered RMSNorm with weight decay on the norm gains, QK-norm — are attributed to Qwen3.5 by the official blog's "stability optimizations" umbrella rather than re-documented per-component; the config confirms only rms_norm_eps 1e-6.

5. MoE — 4% Activation

Every layer's FFN is a mixture of experts: 512 routed experts of intermediate dim 1024, top-10 per token, plus one always-on shared expert. With hidden dim 4096, each routed expert is a thin slice — fine-grained experts in the DeepSeekMoE tradition — and the activated fraction works out to ≈4.3% of total parameters (17B of 397B). The router uses a small auxiliary balance loss (coef 0.001); the shared expert absorbs common-to-all-tokens computation so the routed experts can specialize.

Note what Qwen3.5 doesn't adopt: no aux-loss-free bias balancing (DeepSeek), no quantile-based router (Kimi). The MoE is deliberately boring — the architecture risk budget was spent on attention.

6. Multi-Token Prediction, Trained Multi-Step

One MTP layer (mtp_hidden_layers: 1, no separate MTP embedding) trained with multi-step unrolling — the module predicts several tokens ahead during training, not just one. The dual payoff is the standard one: a denser training signal, and a free self-speculative draft head at serving time — a large part of the blog's headline decode-throughput claims (8.6× vs Qwen3-Max at 32K context, 19× at 256K) is the hybrid stack and MTP drafting compounding.

7. Native Multimodality — Early Fusion

Qwen3.5's actually-new axis. Prior Qwen generations trained a text LLM, then grafted vision on as a separate -VL branch. Qwen3.5 puts a vision encoder inside the base model — a 27-layer, SigLIP-class ViT (hidden 1152, patch 16, projected into the LLM's 4096-dim stream) — and pretrains on mixed text-image-video from the start:

  • Early fusion: visual tokens enter the same transformer stack as text tokens; there is no frozen-encoder + adapter stage. The blog reports mixed-modality pretraining at ≈100% of text-only throughput (and ~50% activation-memory reduction from the broader efficiency work), i.e. multimodality at approximately zero training-throughput cost.
  • Interleaved M-RoPE: the rotary dims are partitioned across (temporal, height, width) — config mrope_section [11, 11, 10] of the 32 rotated pairs — with the interleaved layout preserving pure-text behavior when no vision tokens are present.
  • The claim to beat: the blog reports the natively-fused model outperforming the equivalent-scale separately-trained Qwen3-VL — early fusion at no capability cost to text.

Together with Kimi K3's from-scratch MoonViT-V2, this makes 2026 the year native multimodality became the default for frontier open models — DeepSeek-V4 is now the text-only outlier (its report lists multimodality as future work).

8. Qwen3.5 Against Its Contemporaries

All three 2026 frontier open models converged on extreme-sparsity MoE + MTP + ~1M context, and split on attention: Qwen3.5 and Kimi K3 bet on recurrent state (linear-attention hybrids), while DeepSeek-V4 kept softmax everywhere and compressed the sequence it reads. Qwen3.5 is the smallest and most conservative of the three — no residual-stream redesign, no exotic router — and the only one shipping one architecture from 0.8B to 397B. That conservatism lasted one generation: Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (§1) adds a four-branch gated residual, replaces the full-attention layers with QSA, and moves to Muon.

10. SFT Memory — Resident State Is Only the First Term

A common mixed-precision AdamW budget is 16 bytes per trainable parameter: BF16 weight (2), BF16 gradient (2), FP32 master weight (4), and two FP32 moments (8). Keeping gradients in FP32 instead makes that 18 bytes. FSDP2 can instead retain FP32 parameter shards, FP32 gradients and FP32 moments (16 bytes), materializing BF16 compute weights transiently. These are different layouts with similar headline totals: inspect the actual optimizer and dtype policy, rather than adding a second master copy to an FP32 parameter that already serves that role.

A useful first-pass budget is \(M_{\rm budget}=bP/N+2U+R\) bytes: \(U\) is the largest individually gathered parameter unit and \(R\) is a user-supplied allowance for activations, temporary gradients, communication/workspace buffers and allocator overhead. Here units are individual decoder blocks, the embedding, and the LM head. Thus the largest BF16 unit is 2.37 GiB for 27B (a vocabulary matrix), 1.57 GiB for 35B, and 12.25 GiB for 397B (a decoder block). Prefetching another block can keep a second large unit live.

resident model state + one BF16 unit + allowance usable device memory
397B, 128 ranks: 46.14 GiB resident + 12.25 GiB gathered + 16 GiB allowance = 74.39 GiB.

Adding GPUs shrinks sharded state, not the full unit gathered for computation. This is an arithmetic planning tool for plain full sharding, not an EP/TP/PP simulator or a measured peak-memory prediction. The allowance is not computed from sequence length; it must be replaced with profiling evidence. A budget below the line does not guarantee fit. Use actual available GiB, not a GPU's marketed GB capacity. The table above remains usable without JavaScript.

Do not extrapolate from the inference-cache plot. SFT normally sets use_cache=False. Its memory pressure includes saved mixer/MLP activations, routed token buffers and loss intermediates. Just one BF16 logits tensor at microbatch 1, sequence 8192, vocabulary 248,320 is 3.79 GiB; FP32 doubles that. Use a supported fused/chunked loss that avoids the full live logits/softmax allocation, and verify the actual allocation path rather than trusting the word “fused.”

11. Shard the Expensive Dimension

FSDP and ZeRO shard stored state across data-parallel ranks, then gather weights to compute a module. TP splits matrix computation, PP splits depth, and EP assigns whole experts to owners and moves tokens to them. Sharding the expert tensor with plain FSDP is not expert parallelism: its all-gather reconstructs the bank for that module, even if each token uses only top-k experts.

A. 27B dense: full sharding is the straightforward baseline

On one fast-interconnect 8-GPU node with about 80 GiB usable per device, the 50.10-GiB text-state floor makes FSDP2 / ZeRO-3 a reasonable candidate for short-context full SFT. Start at microbatch 1 and 2K–4K sequence length with activation checkpointing; measure before increasing either. Sixteen GPUs offer substantially more headroom but may add inter-node gathers. There are no routed experts to distribute, so EP has no role.

Use individual decoder blocks as FSDP units, and shard the embedding and LM head separately. Wrapping only the root gathers the entire model; leaving both vocabulary matrices in a large root group can also inflate the live set. Initialize on meta / load shards without first building a full GPU replica. Create the optimizer after sharding.

FSDP2 placement sketch for a text-only causal-LM model

This illustrates boundaries, not a complete launcher or checkpoint loader. It assumes an initialized distributed process group and a text-only Qwen3_5ForCausalLM model; a production trainer must supply shard-aware pretrained loading, the data sampler, loss masking and distributed checkpoint save/resume.

import torch
from torch.distributed.fsdp import fully_shard, MixedPrecisionPolicy

model.config.use_cache = False
model.gradient_checkpointing_enable()
policy = MixedPrecisionPolicy(
    param_dtype=torch.bfloat16, reduce_dtype=torch.float32
)
for layer in model.model.layers:
    fully_shard(layer, mp_policy=policy, reshard_after_forward=True)
fully_shard(model.model.embed_tokens, mp_policy=policy)
fully_shard(model.lm_head, mp_policy=policy)
fully_shard(model, mp_policy=policy)
optimizer = torch.optim.AdamW(model.parameters(), lr=5e-6)

Use FP32 master parameter shards for the 16-byte FSDP2 layout described above. Check the peak with checkpointing and prefetch enabled: recomputation can cause additional parameter gathers, and accumulating gradients without synchronization can increase memory.

B. 35B-A3B: prefer local expert ownership when communication dominates

Eight-way plain full sharding starts at 64.56 GiB per rank before activations; it is a tight 80-GiB full-SFT setup, not “a 3B training job.” A supported MoE training engine can instead keep experts on their owners, use grouped GEMMs, and dispatch tokens. With EP=8, each rank owns 32 of the 256 experts per local decoder layer; each bank is 100,663,296 parameters before shared/dense components. Keep frequent expert traffic inside an NVLink-class domain where possible.

NVIDIA's pinned 16-H100 SFT recipe uses TP=1, PP=2, EP=8, expert-TP=1, with 17 / 23 decoder layers split across stages to account for the vision-side workload. It uses full-layer recomputation and a TE loss path to avoid the large vocabulary buffer. For text-only SFT with no MTP, a 20 / 20 split is a starting hypothesis, not an inherited optimum: measure the embedding, logits and stage times before choosing it.

C. 397B-A17B: combine EP, PP and modest TP

Sixty-four 80-GiB GPUs cannot hold even the ideal 16-byte text state without offload or a lower-memory optimizer: the floor is 92.28 GiB per rank. At 128 GPUs the floor falls to 46.14 GiB, but plain FSDP would repeatedly gather 12.25-GiB blocks. Prefer a model-aware MoE engine and treat this as a multi-node systems problem.

128-GPU flagship layout, with the rank arithmetic exposed

With CP=1, the dense and expert parts partition the same ranks differently:

\[ N=PP\cdot TP\cdot DP_{\rm dense} =PP\cdot EP\cdot TP_{\rm expert}\cdot DP_{\rm expert}. \]

For the flagship preset: \(128=4\cdot2\cdot16=4\cdot32\cdot1\cdot1\). Do not multiply TP × PP × EP and call that the world size. This requires an engine supporting separate dense/expert parallel layouts, not arbitrary combinations of generic distributed flags.

PP=4 starts with 15 decoder layers per stage; EP=32 gives 16 routed experts per layer per expert rank. That is \(15\cdot16\cdot12{,}582{,}912 =3{,}019{,}898{,}880\) routed parameters per rank, or 45 GiB under the 16-byte state assumption, before dense/shared parameters, embeddings, vision, MTP and activations. With expert-DP=1 there is no further expert optimizer-state sharding in this layout.

Use TP=2 within a fast local pair. The flagship has only two KV heads: larger TP requires explicit KV replication or another supported mapping. GDN also needs head-aware partitioning. Its packed projections, convolution channels and head-specific gates must agree on ownership.

EP=32 spans multiple 8-GPU nodes, so all-to-all bandwidth and tail latency matter. If that traffic dominates, compare smaller EP with more expert-DP sharding or a different pipeline split, rather than assuming larger EP is always faster. PP moves boundary activations instead of repeatedly gathering every layer, but introduces bubbles: for a simple balanced non-interleaved schedule, utilization is roughly \(m/(m+PP-1)\) with \(m\) microbatches. Profile real stage times; the last stage may own the LM head and MTP, and the first may own vision.

For longer sequences, sequence parallelism reduces replicated activation work within TP; context parallelism splits sequence computation. Neither is a generic fix for Qwen's hybrid stack. Confirm support for GDN recurrence, convolution boundary exchange, full attention and packed-sample boundaries in the exact backend. Start at CP=1 unless the measured activation limit calls for a verified hybrid-model CP path.

12. An Efficient SFT Run Is More Than a Sharding Flag

Choose full SFT or adapters before choosing the mesh

For a modest instruction dataset or limited hardware, start with a smaller model or LoRA / QLoRA, and compare held-out quality against full SFT. LoRA removes base-weight optimizer states, not the need to store/read the frozen base. An idealized fully sharded BF16-base LoRA budget is \((2P_{\rm frozen}+16P_{\rm adapter})/N\), plus gathered weights and activations. Four-bit storage changes the base term toward \(0.5P_{\rm frozen}\), but scales, unquantized modules and dequantization buffers make the real number larger; confirm that the quantizer supports this model's packed expert tensors and GDN path.

Adapter targeting is architecture-specific. q_proj/v_proj alone cover only the full-attention quarter of the stack. To adapt GDN, consider in_proj_qkv, in_proj_z, in_proj_a, in_proj_b and out_proj; the full-attention path uses q_proj, k_proj, v_proj, o_proj. Scope targets to the language model so a suffix match does not accidentally adapt vision. In the pinned implementation, routed expert gate_up_proj/down_proj are 3D nn.Parameter banks, not ordinary nn.Linear modules. “All linear” therefore need not include them: use a supported PEFT parameter-targeting path or the engine's MoE adapters. Inspect trainable names and gradients. For one linear matrix, rank-\(r\) LoRA adds \(r(d_{\rm in}+d_{\rm out})\) parameters; per-expert adapters multiply that cost by the number of adapted experts.

Make a correct small run, then increase utilization

  1. Pin the checkpoint and stack. Use approved, compatible Transformers/PEFT or Megatron Bridge/Core and kernel versions; do not copy a Qwen3-Next or serving-only TP recipe. Check that pretrained conversion preserves gates, expert ordering, norms, vocabulary matrices and the chosen MTP scope. Use shard-aware load/save/resume so rank 0 does not need a full materialized optimizer checkpoint.
  2. Make the SFT objective explicit. Use the checkpoint's chat template and intended thinking mode; mask system/user/tool/padding tokens when training assistant-only outputs, and retain the intended assistant end tokens. Normalize by actual supervised tokens across ranks and accumulation steps, not by a mean of unequal sequence means. Verify the router auxiliary loss is intentionally enabled and added with the expected scale: router_aux_loss_coef in a config alone does not prove the trainer applies it.
  3. Establish a short-context baseline. Start with microbatch 1 and 2K–4K tokens, BF16 compute, cache disabled and checkpointing. Full-SFT learning rates around \(5\cdot10^{-6}\)–\(2\cdot10^{-5}\) appear in the cited presets, but are starting points, not a universal optimum. Choose steps/warmup from your dataset and monitor held-out quality, gradient norms and finite loss.
  4. Use the actual fast paths. Full-attention FlashAttention/SDPA support is not enough: the 75% GDN path also needs a supported chunked delta-rule backward and fast causal convolution. MoE needs efficient dispatch and grouped GEMMs. Detect fallback warnings; profile loss memory and kernel time before changing parallelism.
  5. Pack only with hybrid-aware isolation. A correct packed batch resets GDN recurrent state and causal-convolution history at sample boundaries, and applies matching boundaries to full attention and labels. An attention mask or reset position IDs alone may not reset the recurrent path. Compare packed vs. separate-example loss; otherwise use length buckets and padding rather than silently leaking context between examples.
  6. Tune against useful throughput. Record non-padding and supervised tokens/s, peak allocated/reserved memory, optimizer-step peak, gather/all-to-all time, pipeline idle time and expert-load imbalance after warmup. Increase microbatch or sequence length one at a time, then compare meshes at the same token budget and objective. Global sequence batch is \(B_{\rm micro}\cdot G_{\rm accumulation}\cdot DP_{\rm dense}\) for the CP=1 layouts above; do not multiply by total GPUs or EP again. Confirm checkpoint resume before scaling out.
Practical decision rule
27B: start with full sharding and measure activations. 35B MoE: compare that baseline with node-local expert ownership. 397B MoE: use a supported EP + PP layout with modest TP and a real network budget. If hardware or data does not justify full SFT, adapters or a smaller checkpoint are usually a better first experiment than a deeply offloaded flagship. Offload can make a run fit, but PCIe/host-memory traffic may erase the throughput advantage.

13. Summary

The recipe

Qwen3-Next's shape, scaled 5×: 3:1 Gated DeltaNet : gated attention, 512-expert MoE at 4% activation, MTP.

Hybrid logic

45 recurrent-state layers carry sequence modeling at \(O(1)\); 15 full layers restore exact recall.

Gated attention

\(\sigma(W_g x) \odot \mathrm{Attn}(x)\) before \(W_O\) — kills attention sinks and massive activations.

New this gen

Native early-fusion vision with interleaved M-RoPE, at ≈zero throughput cost; vocab 248K / 201 languages.

The meta-lesson

Architecture validated on an 80B testbed, then scaled unmodified — de-risked novelty.

Claimed payoff

8.6× decode throughput vs Qwen3-Max at 32K, 19× at 256K; base quality matching a >1T dense-attention predecessor.

Parameter reality

6.575B per DeltaNet block; 6.562B per full-attention block. Text totals 396.346B; vision and MTP are additional.

Training reality

Budget all trainable experts, not just top-k. Sharded state, live gathered units, activations and communication all matter.
