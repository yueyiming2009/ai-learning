Model Architectures

Nemotron 3

NVIDIA's answer to the 2026 frontier: a Mamba-2 hybrid with no positional embeddings anywhere, experts that live in a quarter-width latent space, twenty-two of them active per token — and most linear layers pretrained in 4-bit NVFP4, with the data and recipes published. Spanning the 30B Nano and its training-only successor 3.5 Lightning up to the 550B Ultra.

Nemotron 3 (Nano-30B, December 2025; Super-120B, March 2026; Ultra-550B, June 2026 — joined in August 2026 by the 3.5-generation Lightning-30B) is the Western entry in the frontier open-model class, and the most reproducible one: per-model technical reports, opened pretraining and post-training datasets, and disclosed hyperparameters down to the batch size. It is also a distinct architectural lineage from everything else in this section — the linear layers are Mamba-2 (decay-only SSMs) rather than delta-rule, the experts compute in a compressed latent space, and precision is pushed further than anyone: 4-bit weights not as post-training quantization but in the pretraining loop itself.

1. The Stack — Mamba Between the Meals

Super's 88 layers, verbatim from the config (M Mamba-2, E LatentMoE, * attention):

MEMEMEM*EMEMEMEM*EMEMEMEM*EMEMEMEMEM*EMEMEMEMEM*EMEMEMEMEM*EMEMEMEMEM*EMEMEMEM*EMEMEMEME

Unlike a standard block (mixer + FFN in every layer), Nemotron alternates whole layers: Mamba, MoE, Mamba, MoE — with an attention layer replacing a Mamba slot 8 times (12 in Ultra). Counting token mixers only, that is 40 Mamba : 8 attention — a 5:1 hybrid ratio, leaner than Qwen's and Kimi's 3:1, with a leaner full layer too: 2 KV heads means each attention layer caches just 512 elements per token. The linear layer is also a different animal from the delta-rule family: Mamba-2's transition is a pure decay — one scalar per head, with no erase-write term — KDA without the Householder (its family-tree box places all of these). NVIDIA's bet is that at 5:1 with capable attention layers, the cheaper mixer is enough.

2. No Positional Embeddings At All

Nemotron 3 uses no positional encoding anywhere — not partial RoPE, not learned embeddings. The reasoning is the same one Kimi K3 used to justify NoPE on its global layers, taken to its conclusion: a recurrent layer's causal, decaying state is inherently order-aware, so 40 Mamba layers supply all the positional signal the 8 attention layers need. The payoff is the cleanest long-context story of the generation — extension to 1M tokens is just training at 1M tokens (a late constant-LR phase, mostly 1M-length batches), with no YaRN, no theta retuning, no out-of-distribution rotary frequencies to manage. Three independent 2026 flagships (K3, Nemotron 3, and Meta's iRoPE-style Muse) now run global attention position-free; the direction of travel on the Qwen page's partial-RoPE note has reached its endpoint.

3. LatentMoE — Experts in a Quarter-Width Space

Every FFN layer routes into a latent space at one quarter of the model width: tokens are down-projected 4096 → 1024 (Ultra: 8192 → 2048) before dispatch, the 512 routed experts operate entirely at latent width, and the combined result is up-projected back. The shared expert keeps a full-width path.

Convergent evolution, part three
Kimi K3 — Stable LatentMoE (Jul 2026)

Latent = hidden/2 (7168 → 3584); 896 experts top-16; RMSNorm before up-projection; motive framed as halving all-to-all bytes and expert width.

Nemotron 3 — LatentMoE (Mar 2026)

Latent = hidden/4; 512 experts top-22; ReLU² experts; shipped first, at both 120B and 550B. Same identity: the low-rank logic MLA applies to KV, applied to the FFN.

The second unusual dial: top-22. Against the field's top-6 to top-16, Nemotron activates 22 of 512 experts — many small contributions rather than few large ones, which the latent trick makes affordable: dispatch bytes scale with \(k \times d_{\text{latent}}\), so 22 × 1024 costs less all-to-all traffic than a conventional top-6 × 4096 would. Routing is otherwise the standard recipe — sigmoid scores, aux-loss-free bias balancing (update rate 10⁻³) plus a small conventional balance loss (10⁻⁴) — and the expert activation is ReLU² rather than SwiGLU: no gate branch, one fewer matmul per expert, and a bounded-below activation that NVIDIA has favored since Nemotron-H for its numerics.

4. NVFP4 — Four-Bit Pretraining, Not Four-Bit Packaging

Every model in this section ships low-precision weights; Nemotron 3 is the only one whose pretraining loop ran in 4-bit. Most linear layers compute forward and backward in NVFP4 — FP4 (E2M1) values in 16-element micro-blocks, each block carrying an E4M3 scale under a global FP32 scale — with random Hadamard transforms decorrelating the weight-gradient inputs and stochastic rounding on gradients to keep the estimator unbiased. That is a step beyond K3's MXFP4 QAT (quantization-aware from SFT onward) and GLM-5's INT4-at-SFT: the 25T-token base run itself paid 4-bit prices.

What stayed in higher precision is the interesting part — a map of where 4-bit is still unsafe:

5. MTP — Two Steps, Shared Weights, Attention Inside

The MTP head is two draft steps sharing one weight set — the same weight-tying trick as GLM-5's three-step module, converged on independently — and each step is a miniature hybrid of its own: one attention layer followed by one MoE layer ("*E" in the config). Family-scale ablations measured ≈97% acceptance on the first two draft tokens, making the shipped model self-speculative out of the box.

6. The Optimizer That Didn't Change

Amid the Muon wave — V4, K3, GLM-5 all run Muon variants — Nemotron 3 trains on plain AdamW (wd 0.1, β 0.9/0.95), with a WSD schedule and a minus-sqrt decay over the final 5T tokens. The largest 4-bit pretraining run on record chose the conservative optimizer: worth remembering when Muon starts to look mandatory. The rest of the recipe is likewise disclosed to an unusual degree — batch ~25M tokens, peak LR 4.5e-4 (Super) / 2.5e-4 (Ultra), long-context phases spelled out — and ~3T tokens of the training data are public, which makes this family the one to study when you want to reproduce rather than admire.

7. The Small End — Nano and 3.5 Lightning

The family's first and newest members are the same size. Nemotron 3 Nano (December 15, 2025) opened the generation: a 30B-class MoE (≈31.6B total, ≈3.5B active — branded 30B-A3B) in the same hybrid grammar as the flagships. Nemotron 3.5 Lightning (August 11, 2026) is the first 3.5-generation model, and its config.json is Nano's, value for value — same layer string, same dims, same experts. The half-generation bump is entirely training and packaging; the architecture did not move.

Naming: there is no "Nemotron 3.5 Nano." Lightning is the 3.5-generation replacement for Nano — NVIDIA renamed the small tier (the Hugging Face URL for a 3.5 Nano redirects to Lightning), pitching it as "the knowledge of Ultra packed into the size of Nano."

The shared 52-layer skeleton, verbatim from both configs:

MEMEM*EMEMEM*EMEMEM*EMEMEM*EMEMEM*EMEMEMEM*EMEMEMEME

That is 23 Mamba-2, 23 MoE, and 6 attention layers — a mixer ratio just under 4:1, denser in attention than the flagships' 5:1. Hidden dim 2688; GQA 32 Q / 2 KV at head dim 128; Mamba-2 at 64 heads × dim 64, state 128; vocab 131,072 and the same 1M-token long-context story. The MoE is the interesting departure: 128 routed experts top-6 + 1 shared, expert dim 1856, ReLU² — and no LatentMoE (moe_latent_size: null). The experts run at full model width. At hidden 2688 with top-6 dispatch there is little all-to-all traffic for a latent bottleneck to save; the ¼-width trick of §3 is a big-model economy, and the small models simply skip it.

What that training bought, on the same weights shape: Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index 15 → 24 — level with gpt-oss-120b and two points shy of Super — Terminal-Bench v2.1 7% → 24%, SWE-bench Verified 38.8 → 51.6, MMLU-Pro 78.3 → 81.9. The drafter menu (MTP for medium-to-high concurrency, DSpark for low-concurrency and DGX Spark, DFlash to benchmark against — the mechanics are on the speculative decoding page) is where the "up to 4× output speed" claim lives; hosted endpoints measured ~670 tokens/s at launch.

The positioning is explicit: Lightning is an execution-layer model, meant to sit under a frontier planner and absorb the routine calls that dominate an agent's token budget. It shipped alongside NeMo Switchyard, a router that decides per-request whether the small model suffices — LangChain's 145-task benchmark measured a 74% cost cut versus frontier-only, sending just 7% of calls upstream, at a ~6-point accuracy giveback. The architectural lesson runs the other way: when a skeleton is settled, a whole generation bump can come from data, a precision recipe, and inference packaging — with zero changes to the stack.

9. Summary

The stack

Alternating Mamba-2 / LatentMoE layers, attention every ~11th slot — a 5:1 mixer hybrid with 2-KV-head full layers.

Positions

None. The SSM layers carry order; 1M context = training at 1M, no rescaling machinery.

LatentMoE

Experts at ¼ model width, top-22 of 512 — many small contributions, cheap dispatch. Convergent with K3.

NVFP4

4-bit in the pretraining loop; the BF16 exception list maps exactly where 4-bit still fails.

MTP

2 shared-weight draft steps, each an attention+MoE pair; ≈97% two-token acceptance.

Nano → 3.5 Lightning

Config-identical 30B pair; the generation bump is pure training — NVFP4 pretrain, baked-in MTP, a three-drafter serving kit.

Why study it

Open data, open recipes, disclosed hyperparameters — the reproducible member of the 2026 class.
