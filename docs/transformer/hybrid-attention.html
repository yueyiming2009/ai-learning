Hybrid Attention Stacks

Most layers recurrent, a few layers exact: the design pattern behind Qwen3.5 and Kimi K3 — the ratio, the placement, what the full layers are for, and why two labs independently landed on 3:1.

Two pure designs, two dual failure modes. A stack of softmax attention layers recalls anything, verbatim, at any distance — and pays memory and decode compute proportional to context length in every layer. A stack of linear attention layers runs at constant per-token cost — and stores its entire history in a fixed-size state that is lossy by construction. The hybrid stack is the observation that these failure modes cancel: make most layers cheap recurrent mixers and keep a few exact softmax layers as the random-access path. Nearly every 2025–26 linear-attention deployment at scale — Jamba, MiniMax-01, Qwen3-Next, Qwen3.5, Kimi Linear, Kimi K3 — is a hybrid; pure linear stacks stayed in research papers.

1. The Premise — Recall Is Rare but Non-Negotiable

The empirical bet under every hybrid: most token mixing doesn't need exact retrieval. Local syntax, coreference over a paragraph, topic state, incremental computation — a decaying associative memory handles all of it. What a fixed-size state cannot do is verbatim recall of arbitrary spans at arbitrary distance ("what was the exact value defined 400K tokens ago?") — the capacity analysis shows the state blurs precisely when many items compete. So the architecture rations exactness: a handful of full-attention layers, each seeing the entire uncompressed history, provide lookup; everything else streams.

2. The Design Axes

A hybrid stack is specified by four choices:

  1. The ratio — linear layers per full layer.
  2. The linear layer — which recurrent mixer (Mamba, lightning attention, Gated DeltaNet, KDA).
  3. The full layer — vanilla GQA, gated attention, or MLA; and its positional scheme (RoPE, partial RoPE, NoPE).
  4. The placement — uniform interleave vs. blocks; what the first and last layers are.

Hybrids by conversion

A 2026 pattern worth knowing: hybrids no longer have to be pretrained hybrid. Ant's Ling-2.5-1T — the first trillion-parameter open hybrid — was built by converting a subset of a pretrained model's GQA layers into lightning linear attention (7 of every 8 layers) and continuing training; OpenBMB's MiniCPM-SALA converted a 9B dense model into a 25% trainable- sparse / 75% linear stack for roughly a quarter of from-scratch cost; and ServiceNow's SuperApriel took the idea to its limit — a supernet where each layer can execute any of four mixers (full GQA, sliding window, Gated DeltaNet, KDA), with eight Pareto-optimal placements extractable from one checkpoint. The pretrained softmax weights evidently contain most of what the recurrent replacement needs — which also means the ratio can be chosen after pretraining, not committed to before it.

3. Why a Few Full Layers Are Enough

  • Retrieval structure is concentrated, not uniform. Interpretability work consistently finds that exact-copy behavior (induction heads, retrieval heads) lives in a small subset of heads and layers. Give the model 25% softmax layers and training routes the retrieval-heavy circuits into them; the recurrent layers keep the roles they're good at.
  • One exact read can be re-broadcast. Information retrieved by a full layer enters the residual stream and is available to every layer above it. The stack needs access to exact recall at several depths — not exact recall in every layer.
  • The empirical convergence: Kimi's published ablations (the Kimi Linear line) found 3:1 quality-neutral vs. denser attention, and two labs (Qwen, Moonshot) landed on 3:1 independently. MiniMax's earlier 7:1 traded a little recall robustness for more speed. Nobody ships less than ~12% full attention — recall degrades noticeably beyond that in needle-in-haystack regimes.

4. The Arithmetic of the Ratio

Let a stack have \(L\) layers, of which \(L_f\) are full attention with per-token KV cost \(c_{kv}\), and \(L - L_f\) linear layers with constant state size \(c_s\). Sequence memory:

\[ M(T) \;=\; \underbrace{L_f \, c_{kv}\, T}_{\text{grows with context}} \;+\; \underbrace{(L - L_f)\, c_s}_{\text{fixed floor}} \]

The ratio only moves the slope: 3:1 cuts it 4×, 7:1 cuts it 8×, all-linear zeroes it. Each doubling of the ratio buys half as much as the last — while the recall risk compounds.

The marginal memory saved shrinks geometrically; the recall risk doesn't. That asymmetry is why the deployed frontier clusters at 3:1.

5. Placement Details That Turn Out to Matter

  • Uniform interleave (every \(r{+}1\)-th layer full) beats clustering: each recurrent segment is at most \(r\) layers from an exact read in both directions.
  • The last layer should be global. K3 appends one extra gated-MLA layer so the stack ends on full attention — the final prediction gets one last exact look at the whole context, with no recurrent blur after it.
  • Positions: if the linear layer's decay is channel-wise (KDA), it carries a relative position code by itself and the full layers can run NoPE — which also makes context extension rescaling-free. With scalar-gated linear layers (GDN), Qwen keeps partial RoPE in the full layers instead.
  • Short conv in front of the linear layers (kernel 3–4, depthwise) is universal: the recurrent state shouldn't waste capacity on adjacency the convolution gets for free.

6. Training and Serving Consequences

  • Prefill/training compute: full layers cost \(O(T^2)\) at prefill, linear layers \(O(T)\) via the chunkwise kernels. At 1M-token sequences the few full layers dominate prefill compute — the ratio is doing double duty, cutting the quadratic term 4× too.
  • The cache becomes two objects. Serving stacks built around paged KV must now also checkpoint recurrent states: prefix caching stores per-chunk state snapshots (K3's infra calls this state-aware prefix caching), and preemption/restart must replay or restore states, not just remap pages.
  • Decode is bandwidth-light. The linear layers read a fixed-size state instead of a growing cache — the source of the 8–19× long-context decode throughput gains Qwen3.5 reports, and a large win for RL rollout serving, where long-context decode dominates.

7. Open Questions — and One Public Recantation

The hybrid bet has a prominent dissenter, and it is the lab that made the bet first. MiniMax pioneered lightning-attention hybrids at scale (Text-01, M1) — then reverted to full softmax attention in every layer for M2 and M2.5, with a published post-mortem: hybrid models matched full attention on saturated benchmarks but showed clear deficits on multi-hop reasoning (their read: retrieval-heavy attention patterns form early in pretraining and the efficient layers couldn't reproduce them); linear layers broke the serving stack they cared about (a recurrent state has no prefix cache and complicates speculative decoding — §6's "two objects" problem, experienced in production); and their internal 128K RULER ablation scored full attention 90 vs. 72 for the sliding-window alternative. Their conclusion — "efficient attention still has some way to go before it can definitively beat full attention" — is the strongest published counterweight to this page. Note what it does and doesn't contradict: Qwen and Moonshot's hybrids keep 25% exact layers precisely to protect retrieval circuits; MiniMax's ablation compared against SWA, not a 3:1 delta-rule hybrid. The experiment that would settle it hasn't been published.

  • Does the ratio have a scaling law? 3:1 was tuned at 48–80B; nobody has published whether the optimal ratio drifts with model size or context length.
  • Recall cliffs: hybrids pass standard needle tests, but multi-hop retrieval chains that must route repeatedly through the sparse full layers remain the suspected weak spot — this is exactly where MiniMax reported the damage.
  • State interference at 1M: the linear layers' state carries positional/ordering duty (especially under NoPE) over sequences vastly longer than the state is large; how gracefully this degrades is an active question.

8. Summary

Pattern

Most layers: recurrent state at \(O(1)\)/token. Few layers: exact softmax over everything.

Memory

\(M(T) = L_f c_{kv} T + (L{-}L_f)c_s\) — the ratio sets the slope, the state sets the floor.

The ratio

3:1 captures 75% of the slope win; further thinning buys geometrically less and risks recall — hence the convergence.

Placement

Uniform interleave; end global; conv in front of linear layers; NoPE iff the linear layer encodes position.

Deployed

Qwen3.5 (GDN 3:1, partial RoPE), K3 (KDA 3:1, NoPE), MiniMax-01 (7:1), Jamba (Mamba 7:1).

The alternative

DeepSeek-V4 gets the same division of labor with no recurrence — sparse-selective + heavily-compressed softmax layers.
