Model Architectures

Kimi K3

The largest open model ever released rewrites all four transformer subsystems at once: KDA + gated-MLA hybrid attention with no positional encoding, attention over the residual stream itself, a latent-space MoE with quantile-balanced routing, and Per-Head Muon — trained quantization-aware in MXFP4 to a million tokens of context.

Kimi K3 (Moonshot AI, July 2026 — open weights and a 47-page tech report) is what happens when a lab treats every component of the transformer as up for renegotiation in the same generation. Where Qwen3.5 scaled a proven recipe and DeepSeek-V4 concentrated its novelty in attention, K3 ships new designs for attention, the residual stream, the MoE, and the optimizer — and claims a combined ≈2.5× scaling-efficiency gain over K2 for the trouble.

1. Attention — KDA + Gated MLA, and No Positional Encoding

K3's attention stack composes two Moonshot lineages: the linear-attention layer from Kimi Linear (October 2025) and the MLA lineage from K2 — each upgraded with a gate.

KDA — Kimi Delta Attention

KDA is Gated DeltaNet with the forgetting gate sharpened from a scalar to a channel-wise vector:

The KDA update
\[ S_t = \bigl(I - \beta_t\, k_t k_t^\top\bigr)\,\mathrm{Diag}(\alpha_t)\; S_{t-1} \;+\; \beta_t\, k_t v_t^\top \]

In Gated DeltaNet, \(\alpha_t\) is one scalar per head — the whole memory fades uniformly. In KDA, \(\alpha_t \in (0,1)^{d_k}\) is a vector: each of the state's \(d_k\) key-channels decays at its own input-dependent rate, so one channel can hold an association for thousands of tokens while its neighbor flushes every clause. The state becomes a bank of per-channel timescales rather than one (the full derivation, and why channel-wise decay doubles as a positional code, is on the KDA page). K3's revision over Kimi Linear is at the output: the readout gates are now full-rank (Kimi Linear used a low-rank parameterization) — more expressive silencing of each head's readout.

Gated MLA

The global layers are K2's MLA plus the same sigmoid output gate Qwen3.5 puts on its attention: \(y_t = W_O\bigl[\sigma(W_g x_t) \odot \tilde{o}_t\bigr]\), input-dependent and channel-wise, full-rank. Same motivation, independently adopted: give heads an "output nothing" option (killing attention sinks) and suppress the outlier activations that sabotage low-precision execution — which matters doubly here because K3 trains quantization-aware (§5). During training, attention outputs are accumulated in FP32 with a redesigned kernel, fixing a biased-rounding artifact in stock flash-attention.

The layout, and NoPE

  • 3:1 blocks: 23 blocks of (3 KDA → 1 gated MLA), plus one extra gated-MLA layer at the very top so the final layer always has exact global access — 69 KDA + 24 MLA across 93 layers. The same ratio Qwen validated independently; the why-3:1 economics are on the hybrid attention page.
  • No positional encoding on the MLA layers. No RoPE anywhere in global attention — position information reaches the model only through KDA's causal recurrence, whose per-channel decay is inherently order-aware. This dissolves MLA's decoupled-RoPE workaround (RoPE was the one thing that broke MLA's weight-absorption trick — now it's just gone) and means long-context extension needs no YaRN/rescaling: there is no rotary wavelength to stretch. K3 extrapolates to 1M by simply training there (§7).
Direction of travel across all three 2026 flagships: Qwen3.5 rotates ¼ of dims, DeepSeek-V4 rotates ⅛ (with an output-side correction), K3 rotates none. As recurrent or compressed components take over local ordering, explicit positional encoding is being squeezed out of global attention — and long-context extrapolation gets simpler each step of the way.

2. Block AttnRes — Attention Over Depth

The residual stream normally accumulates uniformly: layer \(\ell\) reads the sum of everything below it, with no say in the mixture. K3 replaces the uniform sum with a learned, input-dependent one — attention over previous layers' outputs:

The mechanism

Each layer forms a learned pseudo-query and takes a softmax over the (RMSNorm'd) outputs of preceding computation blocks, producing a weighted combination as its input — attention along the depth axis instead of the sequence axis. Layer 80 can pull directly and sharply on what layer 20 computed, rather than receiving it diluted through sixty accumulated additions.

Full per-layer AttnRes would cost \(O(L \cdot d)\) per token at \(L = 93\); K3 uses Block AttnRes — the 93 layers are partitioned into 8 blocks of 12, and attention runs over block outputs (9 sources, counting the embedding), cutting the overhead to \(O(N \cdot d)\) with \(N = 8\). This is the second independent 2026 redesign of the residual stream: DeepSeek-V4's mHC widens the stream into 4 lanes with doubly-stochastic mixing; K3 keeps one stream but makes reads selective. Different mechanisms, same diagnosis — uniform residual accumulation wastes the depth axis. Both designs, and the convex-combination constraint they share, are compared on the residual-stream page.

3. Stable LatentMoE — 896 Experts in a Half-Width Space

K3's MoE is 896 routed experts, top-16, plus two full-width shared experts — 56× sparsity. Three design moves make it trainable and serveable at that scale, hence the report's name for it: "Stable LatentMoE".

LatentMoE — route in a compressed space

Tokens are down-projected \(W^{\downarrow}: 7168 \to 3584\) before expert dispatch; routed experts (intermediate dim 3072) operate entirely in the 3584-dim latent space; results are aggregated, RMSNorm'd, and up-projected \(W^{\uparrow}\) back to 7168. Every all-to-all dispatch and combine moves half the bytes, and every expert is half as wide — the same low-rank logic MLA applies to attention's KV path, applied to the FFN. Only the two shared experts keep the full-width path (they never cross the network). The RMSNorm between aggregation and up-projection tames the variance of a 16-expert sum before it re-enters the stream.

SiTU-GLU — a bounded activation for low-precision training

Expert FFNs replace SwiGLU with SiTU-GLU ("Sigmoid-Tanh-Unit GLU"): both the Swish-gate factor and the up-projection branch pass through a softcap \(\beta \tanh(x/\beta)\) — \(\beta_1 = 4\) on the gate, \(\beta_2 = 25\) on the up branch — so no activation can exceed a known bound. DeepSeek-V4 gets the same guarantee by hard-clamping SwiGLU at ±10; K3 builds the bound into a smooth activation instead of a gradient-killing clip. Either way the motive is identical: MXFP4/FP4 training cannot tolerate unbounded activation tails.

Quantile Balancing — aux-loss-free routing without the step size

K3 keeps DeepSeek-V3's sigmoid-scored, bias-based aux-loss-free routing (MoE routing page) but fixes its one hyperparameter. V3 nudges each expert's bias by a fixed step \(\pm\gamma\) per batch — too small and balance lags, too large and loads oscillate. K3 instead sets each bias directly from the quantiles of the current router scores: compute the top-\((k{+}1)\) score cutoffs, place each expert's bias exactly where its target load says it should sit, in one forward pass. No \(\gamma\), no oscillation — the controller becomes a closed-form projection.

Mixtral (2023) activated 28% of its weights; every 2025–26 frontier MoE sits in the 3–5% band — scaling total capacity ~60× while per-token compute grew ~8×. K3 is the current extreme point: 2.78T total, 104B active, 3.7%. The sparsity band is remarkably tight across labs — the activation ratio has quietly become a shared design constant, and mechanisms like latent-space dispatch exist to keep pushing total capacity while holding that band.

4. Per-Head Muon

K2 proved Muon at 1T scale (with MuonClip's QK-clip guarding attention logits); K3 refines where the orthogonalization applies. Muon's Newton–Schulz step orthogonalizes a whole momentum matrix — but a fused QKV projection is really dozens of per-head matrices doing different jobs. Per-Head Muon partitions the momentum along the head dimension and orthogonalizes each head's slice separately: update scale is balanced per head rather than dominated by the loudest one, and the Newton–Schulz iterations run on smaller matrices. K2's weight-clipping mechanism is retained. Embeddings, head, and norms stay on AdamW; cosine LR decay was chosen over WSD via separate scaling-law sweeps.

5. MXFP4 Quantization-Aware Training — Through RL

K3's released weights are MXFP4 (microscaling FP4: 4-bit floats with shared block exponents) for the MoE experts, MXFP8 for their activations, higher precision for attention, latent projections, shared experts, and routers. What makes this notable is when quantization happens: K3 trains quantization-aware through the entire post-training pipeline — SFT and RL. The policy that RL optimizes is bit-identical to the policy that serves: no train/inference mismatch for the quantized model, because there is no separate quantization step after training. (K2 Thinking pioneered the pattern with INT4; K3 moves it to the MX float formats that Blackwell-class hardware executes natively.)

6. MTP, Then EAGLE — the Draft Head Grows Up

K3 pretrains one MTP module (mirroring a backbone block), then does something new after pretraining: the MTP layer is fine-tuned into an EAGLE-3-style draft model for speculative decoding. The target model is frozen; the draft consumes fused low/mid/high features (from the 1st, 4th, and final AttnRes blocks — the depth-attention structure of §2 handing the drafter exactly the multi-level features EAGLE-3 wants) and trains on the LK loss — the negative log of the expected acceptance rate, unrolled 7 draft steps. The MTP head stops being a training-only artifact with a lucky serving use and becomes a first-class, purpose-tuned speculative drafter.

7. Scale, Context, and the Rest of the Report

  • Scaling efficiency: fitted scaling-law curves in the report credit KDA, AttnRes, Stable LatentMoE, and data-recipe refinements jointly with ≈2.5× compute efficiency over the K2 architecture at matched loss.
  • Context curriculum: 8K → 64K during pretraining, 256K → 1M during cooldown — no positional rescaling at any stage (NoPE, §1). Total pretraining token count is undisclosed.
  • Native multimodality: MoonViT-V2, a 401M-param ViT trained from scratch (K2.5's vision tower was SigLIP-initialized), shared weights for image and video with factorized spatial/temporal attention, jointly trained with the LM from the start.
  • Post-training: SFT → per-domain RL experts (one per reasoning-effort level) → multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) into one unified model — the cascade pattern covered on the Cascade RL page, with token-budget rewards controlling effort levels.

8. K3 Against Its Contemporaries

K3 shares the linear-hybrid bet with Qwen3.5 — same 3:1 ratio, delta-rule-family linear layers, gated softmax globals — and differs in aggression: channel-wise gates instead of scalar, NoPE instead of partial RoPE, a redesigned residual stream and router, and 2.5× the total parameters of DeepSeek-V4-Pro. Against V4 it is the clean philosophical contrast: recurrent state vs. compressed summaries as the long-context memory.

9. Summary

Attention

\(S_t = (I - \beta_t k_t k_t^\top)\mathrm{Diag}(\alpha_t)S_{t-1} + \beta_t k_t v_t^\top\) — KDA 3:1 with gated, NoPE MLA.

Residual stream

Block AttnRes: softmax reads over 8 depth-blocks replace uniform accumulation.

MoE

896 experts dispatched in a half-width latent space; SiTU-GLU softcaps; quantile-balanced routing with no step-size knob.

Optimizer

Per-Head Muon — Newton–Schulz per attention head, K2's QK-clip retained.

Numerics

MXFP4 experts / MXFP8 activations, quantization-aware through SFT and RL — serve what you trained.

The claim

≈2.5× scaling efficiency over K2; 2.78T/104B; 1M context with no positional rescaling.
