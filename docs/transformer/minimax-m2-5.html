Model Architectures

MiniMax-M2.5

The control group of the 2026 frontier: the lab that pioneered linear-attention hybrids at scale reverted to full softmax attention in all 62 layers, published exactly why — and spent the saved risk budget on reinforcement learning instead.

Every other page in this section describes a lab making attention cheaper. MiniMax-M2.5 (February 2026, 230B/10B, modified-MIT) is the deliberate exception — and it carries more information than its plain architecture suggests, because MiniMax is not a bystander in the efficient-attention debate. Their Text-01 and M1 models were the first hybrids at frontier scale (7:1 lightning attention). For the M2 series they ran the comparison properly, published the numbers, and went back to full attention everywhere. If the hybrid bet is this site's thesis, M2.5 is the peer review.

1. The Experiment They Ran

Before committing the M2 backbone, MiniMax pretrained matched variants — full attention vs. a sliding-window hybrid (with sink tokens) — and evaluated across knowledge, long-context retrieval, and in-context learning. The table that decided a generation of their models (M2 tech report, Table 2, selected rows):

Three properties of this table matter more than any single number. The damage is invisible on standard benchmarks — a hybrid that matches full attention on MMLU/MATH can be 18 points behind on long-context aggregation. It is length-activated — identical at 32K, broken at 128K. And per the report, it widened after SFT. MiniMax's mechanistic read: retrieval-style attention patterns (the induction/retrieval heads the hybrid page counts on concentrating into the few full layers) form early in pretraining, and the efficient layers never fully reproduce them.

Scope caveat, in both directions: the ablation's efficient arm was a sliding-window hybrid, not a 3:1 delta-rule hybrid à la Qwen3.5 or K3 — SWA is the weakest member of the efficient family (no global state at all). The head-to-head that would settle the debate — full attention vs. a KDA-class hybrid at matched scale, evaluated at 128K+ after post-training — still has not been published by anyone.

2. Why They Reverted — All Three Reasons

  1. Quality, as above — deficits precisely on the multi-hop, long-context, in-context-learning behaviors that agentic workloads live on.
  2. Serving infrastructure. A recurrent state is the two-object cache problem experienced in production: no paged prefix reuse (a coding agent re-reading the same repository re-pays prefill every turn), awkward speculative decoding, and precision-sensitive state numerics. MiniMax's phrasing: efficient attention is "memory-bound, sensitive to low precision" and the surrounding ecosystem is "immature."
  3. The honest asymptote. Their conclusion is dated, not absolute: "efficient attention still has some way to go before it can definitively beat full attention" — with the explicit prediction that the trade flips as GPU memory growth slows. This is a timing disagreement with Qwen and Moonshot, not a physics one.

3. The Price, Knowingly Paid

M2.5 — full attention, 62 layers hypothetical 3:1 hybrid, same geometry

This line is not a mistake — it's the purchase. KV cache vs. context length (BF16; 62 layers × 2048 elements/token at 8 KV heads × 128 dims), against the 3:1 hybrid MiniMax chose not to build (16 full layers + recurrent-state floor). At the 196K native context M2.5 holds ≈50 GB of cache per sequence where the hybrid would hold ≈13 GB — and the 196K cap itself is part of the price: no 1M-context M2.5 exists. What buys it back: only 10B active parameters (decode compute is the frontier's cheapest), 8 KV heads is already 4× leaner than MHA, and every byte of that cache is a losslessly pageable, prefix-cacheable, spec-decode-friendly object — the serving properties reason #2 refuses to give up.

4. The Architecture — Plain, With Deliberate Dials

Calling M2.5 "conventional" undersells three quiet choices in the config:

  • Over-provisioned attention. 48 query heads × 128 dims = 6144 of attention width on a 3072-dim residual stream — attention is 2× wider than the model. Having bet everything on softmax attention, they sized it accordingly; most models keep the ratio at 1×.
  • Half-RoPE at \(\theta = 5\text{M}\), per-layer QK-norm. The same partial-rotation, long-wavelength, normalized-logits package as the hybrids' full layers (Qwen3.5 rotates ¼ at \(10^7\)) — the recipe for stable long-context softmax is now shared across both camps.
  • No shared expert. 256 fine-grained experts, top-8, sigmoid scoring with learnable per-expert bias (the aux-loss-free mechanism, though with a small residual aux loss retained) — and no always-on expert at all, where nearly every contemporary carries one or two. With only 10B active, they spend none of it on common-to-all-tokens capacity.

5. MTP — One Module Trained, Three Shipped

Pretraining used a single DeepSeek-style MTP module (loss 0.3 → 0.1). During the decay phase it was expanded to three: modules 2 and 3 initialized by copying module 1's weights, trained alone against the frozen backbone, then jointly. Compare the two other routes to a deeper drafter on this site — GLM-5 ties three steps to one weight set; K3 fine-tunes its module into an EAGLE drafter — three labs, three retrofits, one conclusion: self-speculation is now standard shipping equipment, and one draft step is not enough.

6. Where the Risk Budget Went

M2.5's novelty lives after pretraining: roughly two months of reinforcement learning across 200,000+ real software environments on their in-house Forge framework — decoupled generation/training in the async-RL mold, process-reward shaping, at a scale of environments that (per the environments landscape) is the actual competitive frontier of 2026. The architecture choice and the RL choice are one decision: a 10B-active full-attention model is cheap to roll out, trivially prefix-cached across multi-turn tool use, and bit-stable under FP8 serving — the least-surprising possible policy for a two-month RL campaign. Every complexity the architecture refused was refused in favor of RL throughput.

8. Summary

Thesis

The pioneer of frontier linear hybrids reverted to full attention — and published the ablation.

The evidence

RULER-128K CWE 90 → 72 under SWA; MTOB in-context translation collapse; zero gap at 32K or on MMLU.

The lesson

Hybrid damage is length-activated, benchmark-invisible, and grows after SFT — evaluate accordingly.

The price

≈50 GB of cache at 196K and no 1M mode — offset by 10B active and a fully cacheable serving path.

The dials

Attention 2× model width; half-RoPE θ=5M; per-layer QK-norm; 256 experts with no shared expert.

The bet

Architecture risk ≈ 0 so RL risk can be maximal: two months in 200K+ environments on Forge.
