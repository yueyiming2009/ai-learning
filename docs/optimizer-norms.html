Optimizers as Steepest Descent Under a Norm

SGD, sign-SGD, Adam, and Muon are not four unrelated tricks — they are the same steepest-descent rule, each measured in a different norm. Choosing an optimizer is choosing a geometry.

The unifying idea

The optimizer zoo looks like a pile of independent heuristics: SGD steps on the gradient, sign-SGD on its sign, Adam divides by a running root-mean-square, Muon orthogonalizes a matrix. They collapse into one equation once you ask: steepest descent — but steep with respect to which norm? Each optimizer is the exact steepest-descent rule for one choice of norm on the weight update. This is the framing of Bernstein & Newhouse, Old Optimizer, New Norm (2024), and it is the mathematical justification behind Muon.

1. Why a norm is even needed

A type mismatch hides inside every update rule. The gradient \(g\) is a covector: it eats a direction \(d\) and returns the first-order change in loss, \(\langle g, d\rangle\). The update \(\Delta w\), however, is a vector — it must live in the same space as \(w\) so we can write \(w \leftarrow w + \Delta w\).

There is no canonical way to convert a covector into a vector. Writing \(\Delta w = -\eta g\) silently assumes the two spaces are identified by the Euclidean inner product. That assumption is a choice of norm, and it is the only place the various optimizers actually differ.

"Just use the gradient" is not geometry-free — it bakes in the \(L_2\) norm. Every optimizer makes this choice; most make it implicitly.

2. Steepest descent — the derivation

Steepest descent takes the step that buys the most loss decrease per unit of size, where size is measured by the chosen norm. Write it as a trust-region (proximal) problem — minimize the local linear model of the loss plus a squared-norm penalty:

\[ \Delta w \;=\; \arg\min_{\Delta}\;\Big[\,\langle g,\Delta\rangle \;+\; \tfrac{1}{2\eta}\lVert\Delta\rVert^2\,\Big] \]
Step A — split into direction and radius

Write \(\Delta = r\,d\) with unit direction \(\lVert d\rVert = 1\) and radius \(r\ge 0\). The objective separates:

\[ \langle g, r d\rangle + \frac{r^2}{2\eta} \;=\; r\,\langle g, d\rangle + \frac{r^2}{2\eta} \]
Step B — optimal direction is the dual-norm oracle

For fixed \(r > 0\), only the \(r\,\langle g,d\rangle\) term depends on \(d\). Minimizing it is the definition of the dual norm:

\[ \min_{\lVert d\rVert = 1}\,\langle g, d\rangle \;=\; -\lVert g\rVert_\ast, \qquad d^\ast \;=\; \arg\min_{\lVert d\rVert = 1}\,\langle g, d\rangle \]

\(d^\ast\) is the linear minimization oracle (LMO) of the norm applied to \(g\) — the steepest unit direction.

Step C — optimal radius

Substitute to get a scalar problem in \(r\), then set the derivative to zero:

\[ \min_{r\ge 0}\;\Big[-r\,\lVert g\rVert_\ast + \tfrac{r^2}{2\eta}\Big] \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; -\lVert g\rVert_\ast + \frac{r}{\eta} = 0 \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; r = \eta\,\lVert g\rVert_\ast \]
Steepest-descent step
\[ \Delta w \;=\; \eta\,\lVert g\rVert_\ast\,d^\ast, \qquad d^\ast = \arg\min_{\lVert d\rVert=1}\langle g,d\rangle \]

Two pieces fall out, and both are fixed by the choice of norm: the direction \(d^\ast\) (the LMO), and the magnitude, which scales with the dual norm \(\lVert g\rVert_\ast\). In practice optimizers keep the direction \(d^\ast\) and set the magnitude from the learning-rate schedule rather than literally multiplying by \(\lVert g\rVert_\ast\).

3. Each optimizer is one norm

Solving the LMO \(\;d^\ast=\arg\min_{\lVert d\rVert\le1}\langle g,d\rangle\;\) for different norms reproduces the canon exactly:

Norm on the updateDual norm \(\lVert g\rVert_\ast\)Direction \(d^\ast\)Optimizer
\(L_2\) (Euclidean)\(\lVert g\rVert_2\)\(-\,g/\lVert g\rVert_2\)SGD / GD
\(L_\infty\) (max coord)\(\lVert g\rVert_1\)\(-\operatorname{sign}(g)\)sign-SGD, Lion
\(L_\infty\) in \(1/\sqrt{v}\)-rescaled coords—\(\approx -\,m/\sqrt{v}\)Adam (approx)
\(L_1\)\(\lVert g\rVert_\infty\)\(-\operatorname{sign}(g_j)\,e_j,\; j=\arg\max_i\lvert g_i\rvert\)greedy coordinate descent
spectral \(\sigma_{\max}\) (matrices)nuclear \(\sum_i\sigma_i\)\(-\,UV^\top\)Muon

\(L_2 \Rightarrow\) SGD

\(\lVert g\rVert_\ast = \lVert g\rVert_2\) and \(d^\ast = -g/\lVert g\rVert_2\), so \(\Delta w = \eta\lVert g\rVert_2\cdot(-g/\lVert g\rVert_2) = -\eta\,g\) — plain gradient descent. The Euclidean norm is the "default geometry" nobody questions.

\(L_\infty \Rightarrow\) sign descent (and Adam)

Under \(\lVert d\rVert_\infty \le 1\) every coordinate is independently free in \([-1,1]\), so \(\langle g,d\rangle=\sum_i g_i d_i\) is minimized coordinate-wise by \(d_i = -\operatorname{sign}(g_i)\). That is sign-SGD, and Lion (sign of the momentum) is its EMA-smoothed cousin. Drop momentum and bias-correction from Adam and its step is \(-\eta\,g/\sqrt{g^2} = -\eta\operatorname{sign}(g)\) — sign descent again, but with each coordinate rescaled by \(1/\sqrt{v}\). So Adam is approximately steepest descent under a coordinate-wise max norm, which is why Adam and sign-SGD behave so similarly in practice.

4. Spectral norm \(\Rightarrow\) Muon

For a weight matrix \(W\), measure update size by its spectral norm \(\sigma_{\max}(\Delta)\) (the largest singular value) rather than treating the entries as a flat vector. The LMO minimizes the matrix inner product \(\langle G,\Delta\rangle = \operatorname{tr}(G^\top\Delta)\) over the spectral-norm ball.

Step A — the minimizer is the orthogonal factor

Take the SVD \(G = U\Sigma V^\top\). Over \(\{\Delta : \sigma_{\max}(\Delta)\le 1\}\), the minimizer of \(\operatorname{tr}(G^\top\Delta)\) is

\[ \Delta^\ast = -\,U V^\top \]

— the gradient with all its singular values flattened to 1, keeping only the rotation/reflection factors.

Step B — verify it hits the dual (nuclear) norm

With \(G^\top = V\Sigma U^\top\) and \(\Delta^\ast = -UV^\top\):

\[ \operatorname{tr}(G^\top\Delta^\ast) = -\operatorname{tr}(V\Sigma U^\top U V^\top) = -\operatorname{tr}(V\Sigma V^\top) = -\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma) = -\sum_i \sigma_i = -\lVert G\rVert_{\text{nuclear}} \]

which equals \(-\lVert G\rVert_\ast\), confirming \(\Delta^\ast\) is the steepest direction (the dual of the spectral norm is the nuclear norm).

Muon update
\[ \Delta W \;\propto\; -\,U V^\top \;=\; -\,(G G^\top)^{-1/2}\,G \]

This is Muon: orthogonalize the momentum matrix and step along it. Computing the SVD every step would be far too expensive, so Muon approximates \(U V^\top\) — the "matrix sign" function \((G G^\top)^{-1/2}G\) — with a few iterations of the Newton–Schulz matrix-polynomial recurrence, which needs only matmuls and no decomposition.

Muon applies only to 2-D parameters (the weight matrices). Embeddings, biases, and norm gains are 1-D and have no meaningful singular-value structure, so those stay on AdamW.

5. Why the norm choice is not cosmetic

The right norm encodes the geometry the parameters actually live in — it is not bookkeeping:

  • Spectral norm controls the layer's behavior. For a weight matrix, \(\sigma_{\max}\) bounds how much the layer's output can move for a bounded-norm input — it is the layer's Lipschitz constant w.r.t. activations. Descending under the spectral norm therefore bounds the change in features uniformly across directions, instead of letting a few large singular directions dominate the update (which is exactly what flat Frobenius / \(L_2\) does).
  • Composed norms give width/depth-robust learning rates. Measure each layer in its own natural norm and the "modular norm" (Bernstein et al.) glues them into one network-wide norm; steepest descent under it yields learning rates that transfer across model sizes — for free, rather than by tuning.

Summary

One equation

\(\Delta w = \eta\lVert g\rVert_\ast\,d^\ast\) — direction = LMO, magnitude = dual norm.

L₂ → SGD

\(d^\ast=-g/\lVert g\rVert_2\), recovering \(-\eta g\).

L∞ → sign / Adam

\(d^\ast=-\operatorname{sign}(g)\); Adam ≈ the same in \(1/\sqrt{v}\) coords.

Spectral → Muon

\(d^\ast=-UV^\top\), the orthogonalized gradient (Newton–Schulz).

Takeaway: Adam ≈ sign-SGD is no accident (same norm), Muon is the principled steepest-descent rule for matrix-shaped parameters, and the frontier of optimizer design is increasingly "pick the norm that respects the architecture, then read off the LMO."
